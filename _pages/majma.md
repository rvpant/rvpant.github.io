[Majma]() is named after _Majma-ul-Bahrain_, or "The Confluence of Two Seas" in English -- the Mughal prince Dara Shikoh's Sufi text exploring the interactions and affinities between Hindu beliefs (Vedanta) and Islam in the subcontinent. Written in Persian, its Hindi translation titled _Samudra Sangam Granth_ inspires the inclusion of the word _sangam_ at the top of the site, alongside the Urdu text reading _majma_. These words both evoke spiritual sentiment, and point to an age of deep religious thought and interchange across the subcontinent. The execution of Data Shikoh at the hands of his brother Aurangzeb is one of many "what-if" moments in the history of the region, and ended an era of fascinating syncretism under Akbar, Shah Jahan, and the Sufi traditions that inspired Dara.

Majma enables one-shot OCR/transcription and translation of historical (and contemporary) documents: primarily Urdu text for now, with imminent extension to Persian, Hindi, and hopefully Punjabi coming soon. I was motivated to build something like this after struggling for years to share bits and pieces of Urdu short stories, news articles, or poetry with my family and friends. There is a vast amount of knowledge, culture, and history "hidden behind the veil" of the Urdu script for many Indians and Indian-Americans like myself. Two introductory examples are shared on the website:
- a page from an Urdu publication of the _Bhagvad Gita_, from Kashmir
- a page from _Krishan Katha_, an article written in praise and in defense of the Hindu god Krishna, by Khwaja Hasan Nizami of Delhi


The history and politics of the subcontinent are increasingly divorced from reality, and communal divisions are erasing peoples' knowledge of the past particularly in the diaspora. My hope in creating this website is to build something that can stop this decline. By increasing access to old texts in Urdu and other languages, we can better know and tell the story of the subcontinent and its unique past at the confluence of two vast seas.
